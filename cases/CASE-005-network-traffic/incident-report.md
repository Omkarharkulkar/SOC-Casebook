## Alert scenario

A network sensor flagged unusual traffic involving a host. The analyst was asked to examine the capture, identify relevant hosts and communication patterns, assess activity requiring further investigation, and document an evidence-supported conclusion.

This is a simulated analyst prompt, not an alert generated by the dataset publisher or an operational IDS/SIEM.

## Executive summary

The capture contains DNS, HTTP, TLS, SMB2, DCE/RPC, and NTP traffic involving two primary internal hosts and several other internal systems.

The most notable observation is repeated SMB activity involving multiple internal destinations, including repeated references to the filename `q4-summary.bin`. SMB CREATE requests for the same filename were observed across multiple internal destinations. The pattern warrants review, but the available PCAP does not establish the file contents, the purpose of the activity, whether a transfer completed, or whether the behavior was unauthorized.

DNS and TLS activity includes connectivity-check and Windows-update-related domains. NTP exchanges are observed between two internal hosts and an internal time server. These observations provide benign context and should not independently be treated as indicators of compromise.

**Verdict:** Suspicious coordinated internal activity observed — further investigation required. The evidence is insufficient to confirm compromise or malicious intent.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — DNS activity

**Observation**

- 710 DNS frames: 355 queries and 355 responses.
- All observed response codes were NOERROR.
- No NXDOMAIN responses were observed.
- All 355 queries were A-record queries.
- 10 queried names were observed.

Queries included:
- `www[.]msftconnecttest[.]com`
- `download[.]windowsupdate[.]com`
- `definitionupdates[.]microsoft[.]com`
- `adl[.]windows[.]com`
- Internal service names under the defanged domain `arden[.]local`

**Assessment**

Connectivity testing and Windows-update-related DNS queries are consistent with ordinary Windows activity. Internal service-name lookups also appear in the capture.

The query volume is higher than Part A, but Part A is much shorter and contains only a limited baseline. DNS volume alone does not establish malicious activity.

**Disposition:** Contextual observation; no standalone DNS IOC identified.

### Finding 2 — HTTP and TLS

**Observation**

HTTP requests include connectivity-test activity and internal service hosts. TLS SNI observations were:

| SNI (defanged) | Observations |
|---|---:|
| definitionupdates[.]microsoft[.]com | 22 |
| adl[.]windows[.]com | 22 |
| download[.]windowsupdate[.]com | 17 |

The capture contains 438 TLS frames and 292 HTTP frames.

**Assessment**

The observed TLS SNI overlaps with Windows update-related DNS activity. The capture does not provide enough evidence to classify these connections as malicious based on destination names or volume alone.

**Disposition:** Contextual observation; no confirmed malicious HTTP/TLS activity.

### Finding 3 — NTP

**Observation**

- 28 NTP packets, representing 14 request/response pairs.
- Two internal hosts communicate with internal time server `TIME-SERVER-1`.
- UDP destination port 123 is used for requests.
- Responses are observed for the listed requests.
- Activity occurs throughout the capture.

**Assessment**

The exchanges are consistent with routine time synchronization. No anomalous NTP behavior was identified from the reviewed metadata.

**Disposition:** No suspicious NTP behavior identified.

### Finding 4 — SMB and DCE/RPC activity

**Observation**

- 782 SMB2 frames.
- 292 DCE/RPC frames.
- RPC interface metadata included `svcctl`, `srvsvc`, and `samr`.
- SMB metadata included repeated references to `q4-summary.bin`.
- The filename was observed in CREATE requests involving multiple internal destinations during two separate periods.
- Corresponding CREATE responses indicated success.

**Assessment**

The multi-host pattern and repeated file-name activity warrant further investigation. However, the presence of SMB, DCE/RPC interfaces, and SMB CREATE requests does not independently prove malicious service activity, unauthorized file access, successful file transfer, execution, or compromise.

The capture does not establish the contents or business purpose of the referenced file.

**Disposition:** Suspicious pattern; further endpoint and authorization context required.

### Finding 5 — Internal host communication

Conversation summaries show sustained communication between the two primary internal hosts and multiple internal destinations. Some internal conversations span a substantial portion of the capture.

The capture also contains traffic to `198[.]18[.]42[.]52`, an address in the reserved benchmarking range. This is not a normal globally routable public IP address and should not be treated as an external threat indicator without additional context.

**Assessment**

The traffic distribution is consistent with the multi-behavior scenario represented by the synthetic dataset. The precise purpose and authorization of the internal activity remain unconfirmed.

**Disposition:** Correlation context; investigate alongside SMB observations.

## Recommended follow-up

1. Confirm whether the source hosts and destinations are expected to communicate over SMB in this environment.
2. Correlate the relevant time periods with endpoint telemetry and host security logs.
3. Confirm whether the repeated file references are associated with an approved workflow.
4. Review available network and endpoint alerts for related activity.
5. Reassess severity if independent evidence establishes unauthorized access, execution, or other harmful activity.

These are investigation recommendations. The PCAP alone does not establish that the actions occurred maliciously.

## Additional Investigation Findings

### SMB file-operation pattern

Two bursts of SMB CREATE requests for `q4-summary.bin` were observed from
HOST-1.

| Observation | First burst | Second burst |
|---|---|---|
| IST | 19:42:41.224455 | 20:00:14.583870 |
| UTC | 14:12:41.224455 | 14:30:14.583870 |
| Destination hosts | 5 | 6 |
| Associated WRITE records | 5 | 6 |

The bursts were approximately 17 minutes and 33 seconds apart. Eleven WRITE
records were observed on the same TCP streams as the corresponding CREATE
requests. Each reported a WRITE length of 96 bytes, giving 1,056 bytes in
total.

The CREATE disposition was `1` (`FILE_OPEN`), indicating a request to open
an existing object. The metadata does not establish whether every write
completed successfully or what content was involved.

The observed SMB share names included `ADMIN$` and `IPC$`. `C$` was not
identified in the extracted share-name results.

### L1 escalation decision

**Decision: Escalate to L2 for validation.**

The repeated SMB activity from one source to multiple destinations, involving
the same filename and associated WRITE requests, warrants further review.
However, this is not confirmation of malicious activity or compromise.

L2 should compare the activity with approved administration, deployment,
backup, or file-distribution tasks and correlate it with endpoint and
authentication logs.

### ATT&CK candidate

**T1021.002 — Remote Services: SMB/Windows Admin Shares**

The observed SMB share activity and multi-host access pattern make this a
candidate for investigation. The available evidence does not establish
unauthorized lateral movement, so this mapping remains a hypothesis.

### Detection hypotheses

1. Identify a source host issuing CREATE requests for the same filename
   across multiple destination hosts within a short period.
2. Correlate repeated SMB access to administrative shares with endpoint
   process creation and remote logon events.
3. Review repeated SMB WRITE requests across multiple internal hosts against
   approved maintenance and deployment activity.

### What could change the assessment?

An approved change record and matching endpoint activity could support a
benign explanation. Unexpected account use, unauthorized remote logons,
confirmed unauthorized writes, or correlated process execution could
increase concern.

The available PCAP does not establish file contents, successful execution,
persistence, exfiltration, or compromise.

### Privacy and evidence handling

Internal addresses and account identities should not be treated as public
indicators. Account names and domains were not included in this report.
